The budget nobody hands you

When a child needs therapies, equipment, and extra care, the costs do not arrive as one number on one page. They arrive a session at a time, a device at a time, a school year at a time. Each piece can feel manageable on its own. Added up across a year, they tell a different story - one that families live but rarely get to show anyone else.

This tool is a way to make that story visible. Choose the supports that sound like your family, or the family you love, and watch the year build. Then see how insurance, Medicaid waivers, and grants can carry a real share of it, and where a nonprofit like Teagan's Crown steps into the gap that is left.

Behind the number

A year is not a spreadsheet. It is a standing frame by the front door, a therapist your child high-fives on Thursdays, a device that finally lets them say "more."

Whatever total you landed on, remember what it is really made of: hours in waiting rooms, phone calls with insurers, forms filed and re-filed, and a parent quietly doing the math again at the kitchen table. The cost is real. So is the child. No family should have to choose between the two, and none should have to carry it alone.

How to read what you just saw

The point of this explorer is not the exact figure - it is the shape of the year. A few honest things to hold onto:

  • Ranges, not prices. A therapy hour, a wheelchair, or a communication device can vary by thousands of dollars depending on where you live and what your child needs. We show that as a low-to-high range on purpose.
  • Some costs repeat, some do not. Therapies tend to recur week after week, while a piece of equipment may be a periodic or one-time purchase - though growing children often need refits and replacements sooner than families expect.
  • Help is real, and it stacks. Insurance, Medicaid and waivers, grants, and nonprofits are not either-or. Families routinely combine them, and each one applied moves the remaining number down.
  • The gap is the story. Even with good coverage, families are often left with a real amount to carry. That gap is the reason organizations like ours exist.

If your family is in this right now

Start with your care team and your plan, keep copies of everything, and pursue more than one funding path at the same time so your child is never waiting on a single answer. When you hit the gap, reach out to us - it is exactly what we are here for.

Questions families ask

Honest answers about the numbers.

Are these real prices for my child?

No. Every figure in this explorer is an illustrative estimate built from broad, publicly discussed cost ranges, not a quote for your child. Real costs vary enormously by state, plan, provider, device, and your child's specific needs.

The purpose here is to make the shape of a year visible, not to predict your bill. Always verify actual costs with your providers, your equipment supplier, and your insurer.

Why show ranges instead of one exact number?

Because one exact number would be a fiction. A therapy session, a wheelchair, or a communication device can differ by thousands of dollars depending on where you live and what your child needs.

A range is the honest way to show that a cost is real without pretending we know your family's precise figure. Treat the totals as a conversation starter, not a price tag.

Will insurance or Medicaid really cover this much?

Often a meaningful share, yes, though the details decide everything. Private plans frequently cover therapies and durable medical equipment when they are documented as medically necessary, usually with a prescription and a letter of medical necessity. Medicaid covers medically necessary equipment for children, and many states run Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers that help further.

Coverage, cost-sharing, prior authorization, and waitlists vary by plan and by state, so the offsets in this tool are illustrative only. Confirm what applies to you with your insurer and your state Medicaid office.

What if we are caught in the gap between what is covered and what we can pay?

That gap is exactly where families most often get stuck, and it is exactly why Teagan's Crown exists. You can appeal an insurance denial, and a stronger letter of medical necessity often changes the outcome. Alongside an appeal, families combine grants, equipment-lending closets, and nonprofits that help fill the gap.

If your family is caught in that space, we would be honored to hear from you. Apply for help here.

Can I share this with someone who does not understand what this costs a family?

Please do. This page was built to be shared. Sometimes the fastest way to help a grandparent, a friend, an employer, or a lawmaker understand daily life with complex needs is to let them move the toggles themselves and watch the year add up.

Use the share buttons on the side of the page, or copy the link and send it to whoever needs to see it.
