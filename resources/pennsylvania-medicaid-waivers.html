Pennsylvania Medicaid Waivers for Children (Without the Jargon)

Pennsylvania has one of the most family-friendly paths in the country to a Medicaid card for a child with disabilities, and this is your map through it.

PH-95
PA's "Loophole" category that ignores parent income when a child qualifies on their own disability
3 ODP waivers
Consolidated, Community Living, and P/FDS cover long-term supports for intellectual disability or autism
PUNS
The statewide registry that logs your child's need so their place in line is documented
EPSDT
Federal rule that makes children's Medicaid cover any medically necessary treatment, even beyond the standard plan

The Real Order of Operations

1
1. Call your County Assistance Office (CAO)Apply for Medical Assistance for your child using the PH-95 ("Loophole") category. This is the fastest door in - a child can get a Medicaid card based on their own disability, not your household income. You can start online at COMPASS (compass.state.pa.us).
2
2. Get the Medicaid card first, waiver secondThe PH-95 card unlocks EPSDT, which covers medically necessary therapies, equipment, nursing, and more. Many families get real coverage here before any waiver is approved. Don't wait on a waiver to use this.
3
3. Register with your county / Administrative EntityFor long-term supports, contact your county's Intellectual Disability / Mental Health (ID/MH) office, also called the Administrative Entity (AE). Ask for intake and eligibility for ODP services. They assign a Supports Coordinator.
4
4. Get on PUNSAsk to be added to the PUNS registry (Prioritization of Urgency of Need for Services). This documents your child's need and urgency level. Waiver capacity is limited, so being logged early and accurately matters.
5
5. Build the plan with your Supports CoordinatorOnce a waiver slot is offered, your SC helps write the Individual Support Plan (ISP) - the document that turns approval into actual services, hours, and equipment.

Two different systems, and why families get confused

Pennsylvania actually runs two things people lump together as "the waiver." First is Medical Assistance (Medicaid) itself - the insurance card. Second are the Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers - special programs that pay for long-term supports most insurance won't touch, like in-home habilitation, respite, and behavioral supports.

Here's the part many families miss: your child may not need a waiver to get a Medicaid card. Through the PH-95 category (nicknamed the "Loophole"), a child with a qualifying disability can get Medical Assistance based on their own situation, with parental income and assets disregarded. That card alone, powered by EPSDT, covers a huge amount. The waivers are a second layer on top for ongoing daily supports.

PH-95: the "Loophole" every PA family should know

PH-95 is the category on a PA Medical Assistance application that lets a child qualify on their own disability determination rather than family finances. It's why a two-income family can still get their disabled child a Medicaid card. You apply through your County Assistance Office or online via COMPASS, and the child's disability is reviewed by the state's Medical Review Team.

This is usually the single most valuable step you can take, and it does not require you to be low-income. If a caseworker steers you away because of your salary, politely ask specifically about the PH-95 / disability category for the child.

The three ODP waivers (for intellectual disability or autism)

Pennsylvania's Office of Developmental Programs (ODP) runs three waivers for people with an intellectual disability or autism whose onset was before age 22: the Consolidated Waiver, the Community Living Waiver, and the Person/Family Directed Support (P/FDS) Waiver.

In plain terms: Consolidated is the most comprehensive with no annual spending cap and is often the route when needs are intensive; Community Living and P/FDS carry annual funding limits and tend to move faster for children with lower-cost support needs. Your Supports Coordinator helps match your child to the right one - you don't have to figure this out alone.

These waivers require a qualifying ID or autism diagnosis. A child with a physical or medical disability but no intellectual disability may not fit ODP - which is exactly why the PH-95 Medicaid card and EPSDT matter so much as the foundation.

What EPSDT actually forces Medicaid to cover

EPSDT stands for Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic and Treatment. It's a federal requirement, and for children on Medicaid it is powerful: if a service is medically necessary to treat or improve your child's condition, Medicaid must cover it - even if it isn't in the standard adult benefit package.

In practice that means therapies (PT, OT, speech), private-duty nursing, augmentative communication devices, wheelchairs and standers, feeding supplies, and more can be covered when a doctor documents medical necessity. Denials happen, but EPSDT gives you strong footing to appeal, because the standard is your individual child's need, not a one-size checklist.

The waitlist is real - here's how to work it

Waiver slots are limited and Pennsylvania does not fund every eligible child immediately. The PUNS registry is how the state tracks who needs services and how urgently. Getting registered, and keeping your urgency level accurate as your child's needs change, is one of the few things in your control.

Update PUNS whenever something changes - a new diagnosis, a hospitalization, a surgery like spinal fusion, loss of a caregiver, or a safety risk at home. An out-of-date PUNS can leave your child looking less urgent than they truly are. Ask your Supports Coordinator to review it with you at least once a year.

Who to call, in one place

County Assistance Office (CAO) or COMPASS: to apply for the Medical Assistance card under PH-95. County ID/MH office (Administrative Entity): to start ODP intake, get a Supports Coordinator, and get onto PUNS.

If you feel lost, two neutral guides can walk beside you free of charge: your county's ID/MH intake line, and Pennsylvania's PEAL Center (a parent training and information center) for help understanding rights, denials, and appeals. You do not have to master the acronyms before you call - that's literally their job.

Get the card before the waiver

Don't sit on the sidelines waiting for a waiver slot. Apply for the PH-95 Medicaid card now - through EPSDT it can start covering therapies, equipment, and nursing right away, while your waiver request works through the system. Two separate lines, two separate calls, start both today.

Frequently asked questions

We're not low-income. Can our child still get PA Medicaid?
Very likely yes. Through the PH-95 ("Loophole") category, a child qualifies on their own disability and parental income is disregarded. Many working, middle-income PA families have a Medicaid card for their child this way. Apply through your County Assistance Office or COMPASS and ask specifically about the disability category for the child.
What's the difference between the Medicaid card and "the waiver"?
The card is health insurance (Medical Assistance) and, through EPSDT, already covers a lot of medically necessary care for kids. The HCBS waivers are an added layer paying for long-term daily supports like in-home help, habilitation, and respite. You can - and usually should - get the card first while pursuing a waiver.
My child has cerebral palsy but not an intellectual disability. Do the ODP waivers apply?
The three ODP waivers require a qualifying intellectual disability or autism. A child with a physical or medical disability alone may not fit them. That's exactly why the PH-95 Medicaid card plus EPSDT is so important - it can cover therapies, equipment, and nursing without an ODP waiver.
How long is the PA waiver waitlist?
It varies by county, by waiver, and by your child's documented urgency - there's no single statewide number, and it changes over time. What you can control is being registered on PUNS accurately and keeping your urgency level current whenever needs increase.
Medicaid denied a therapy my doctor ordered. Now what?
Don't stop at the denial. For children, EPSDT sets the standard at medical necessity for your individual child, which is strong ground for appeal. Get the denial in writing, ask your doctor for a letter of medical necessity, and file the appeal within the stated deadline. Pennsylvania's PEAL Center can help you through it.
Where do I actually start today?
Call your County Assistance Office (or start at compass.state.pa.us) to apply for Medical Assistance under PH-95, and separately call your county ID/MH office to begin ODP intake and get on PUNS. Doing both in parallel is normal and smart.
