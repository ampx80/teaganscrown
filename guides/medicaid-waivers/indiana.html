Medicaid waivers for children with disabilities in Indiana

What Medicaid waivers are, what they typically help families with, why waitlists are common, and the honest first steps to begin, in plain language.

Apply for help Official resources

What a Medicaid waiver is

If you are raising a child with a disability in Indiana, you have probably heard other parents talk about "the waiver." It can sound like insider language, but the idea underneath it is simple. A Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services waiver, usually shortened to an HCBS waiver, is a program that helps a child receive care and support at home and in the community rather than in an institution such as a hospital or care facility.

These waivers are authorized under federal law, but they are designed and run by each individual state. That is the single most important thing to understand as an Indiana family. The broad framework comes from the federal government, while the specific programs, the services they cover, the eligibility rules, and the way you apply are all set by the state. Because of that, the details in Indiana can differ from what a friend or relative describes in another state, and the only fully reliable source for Indiana specifics is the official state agency.

The word "waiver" refers to the fact that the state is allowed to waive, or set aside, some of Medicaid's usual rules in order to serve people with significant needs in their own homes. For many families, that flexibility is what makes it possible to keep a child at home, supported and thriving, surrounded by the people who love them.

A waiver is not charity and it is not a loan. It is a public program built on the belief that children with disabilities belong at home and in their communities.

What Medicaid waivers typically help with

Exactly what a waiver covers depends on the specific program, and Indiana sets its own covered-service lists. In general terms, though, HCBS waivers across the country commonly help families with supports like these:

  • Respite care so a parent or caregiver can rest, work, or attend to the rest of the family while their child is cared for safely.
  • Therapies that support a child's development and daily functioning, alongside other coverage a child may have.
  • Personal care and attendant support with everyday activities such as bathing, dressing, eating, and mobility.
  • Adaptive equipment and home or vehicle modifications that help a child participate more fully and safely in daily life.
  • Case management to help coordinate services and guide families through the system.

Think of a waiver as a way to wrap practical, day-to-day support around a child and family. It is not meant to replace a child's medical insurance or standard Medicaid coverage. Instead it fills in the community-based help that keeps a child growing and a family steady. For the precise menu of services offered in Indiana, and any limits that apply, check with the official state agency listed below.

Why waitlists are common

Here is an honest note that helps many families plan. Waivers often have a limited number of funded slots, which means that in many states more families apply than there are openings at a given time. When that happens, families are placed on a waiting list until a spot becomes available.

Waitlists are a normal part of how these programs work, and being placed on one is not a rejection. It simply reflects demand. Because timing can be uncertain and policies change, the practical takeaway is to reach out and begin the process as early as you reasonably can, so your child's name is in the system while you continue to line up other support. Do not assume a waitlist means the door is closed; ask the state agency what your options are while you wait.

How to begin

Every family's path is a little different, but the general starting steps look much the same:

  1. Contact the state agency. Reach out to the official Indiana Medicaid or developmental-disabilities agency and tell them you are seeking waiver services for your child with a disability.
  2. Request a screening or intake. Ask about a waiver screening or eligibility intake. This is the step where the agency learns about your child's needs and explains which programs may fit.
  3. Gather documentation. Expect to provide information about your child's diagnosis, needs, and household. The agency will tell you exactly what they require.
  4. Ask about the waiting list. If a relevant waiver has a list, ask how to get your child's name on it as early as possible.
  5. Keep records and follow up. Save copies of everything, note who you spoke with and when, and stay in regular contact as your application moves along.

Because eligibility rules, program names, income limits, and waitlist timing are all set at the state level and can change, we are deliberately not listing those specifics here. The right numbers and program details for your family come straight from the official Indiana sources in the next section, and from a knowledgeable case manager or your local parent center.

A quick word while you wait

Applying for a waiver and waiting for a slot can take time, and life does not pause in the meantime. Questions families ask

Answers, in plain language.

What is a Medicaid HCBS waiver?

A Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver is a Medicaid program, authorized under federal law and run by each state, that helps a person with a disability receive care and support at home and in the community instead of in an institution.

Waivers can help cover services like respite for caregivers, therapies, personal care, and certain equipment. Because each state designs and administers its own waivers, the exact programs, covered services, and rules vary from state to state.

What do Medicaid waivers typically help children and families with?

It depends on the specific waiver, but HCBS waivers commonly help with things such as respite care so caregivers can rest, personal care and attendant support, therapies, case management, home or vehicle modifications, and certain adaptive equipment.

The goal is to support a child living and growing at home with their family. For the exact list of covered services in Indiana, check with the official state agency.

Is there a waitlist for Medicaid waivers?

Often, yes. Many states operate waivers with a limited number of slots, so families are frequently placed on a waiting list until a spot becomes available.

Because of this, it is wise to ask about applying and getting on any relevant list as early as you can. Waitlist policies and timing vary and change over time, so confirm the current situation with the official Indiana agency.

How do I start applying for a Medicaid waiver in Indiana?

In general terms, families begin by contacting their state Medicaid or developmental-disabilities agency and requesting a waiver screening or intake. From there the agency explains which programs your child may be eligible for, what documentation is needed, and how to get on any waiting list.

Because eligibility rules and steps are set at the state level, use the official Indiana Medicaid and developmental-disabilities resources for the specifics that apply to your family.

Does my family's income determine waiver eligibility?

Eligibility for Medicaid waivers is based on rules set by each state and can consider both the child's disability-related needs and financial factors. Some waivers are designed to reach children who might not qualify for standard Medicaid by household income, but the details differ by program and change over time.

The only reliable way to know your child's eligibility is to ask the official state agency directly.
